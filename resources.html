Practical tools.
Use what helps.

Simple, non-clinical tools for clearer conversations, decisions and self-regulation.

Communication

Difficult conversation prep

  1. Write the observable facts in one or two sentences. Leave motives out.
  2. Name what seems important to the other person, tentatively rather than as fact.
  3. Choose one thing you genuinely want to understand.
  4. Decide what you need to say clearly without demanding agreement.
  5. Choose one realistic next step you could both actually take.

Decision-making

Decision clarity worksheet

  • Facts: What do I actually know?
  • Assumptions: What am I filling in?
  • Costs: What does each option require?
  • Values: What matters most here?
  • Reversible move: What can I test without locking myself in?
  • Deadline: When does a decision actually need to be made?

Self-regulation

90-second reset

  1. Stop adding new information for a moment.
  2. Feel your feet, chair or another stable physical contact point.
  3. Let your exhale be slightly longer than your inhale for several breaths.
  4. Name three things you can see and one sound you can hear.
  5. Ask: “What is the next useful action—not the whole solution?”

This is a general grounding exercise, not a substitute for medical or mental-health care.
